- **🔥 [EPSBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Earnings Forecasting](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38293)** (`2 upvotes`)
  - *Summary*: Quarterly earnings forecasting requires an agent to connect financial evidence to the income-statement line items that produce a company's reported earnings. We introduce EPSBench, a benchmark for studying this ability across 149 firm-quarters from 86 US companies. Agents read a fixed collection of filings, earnings-call transcripts, and news, then estimate company-specific income-statement drivers with cited evidence. A shared accounting calculator converts these estimates into GAAP diluted earnings per share (EPS), providing a common basis for comparing forecast assumptions. We evaluate six models under a common harness, assessing point forecast error relative to date-matched analyst consensus and checking whether their prediction intervals achieve the stated coverage. Three models show no detectable paired difference in point forecast error from consensus, and three have higher error. Across all six models, nominal 80% intervals contain reported EPS in only 37--70% of runs that return an interval, and most misses fall above the upper bound because the forecasts run low. The driver records trace most of the error gap between models to operating-income estimates.

- **🔥 [Weaken to Strengthen: Weakening-Induced On-Policy Self-Distillation for Large Reasoning Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38681)** (`2 upvotes`)
  - *Summary*: Self-distillation offers a way to improve reasoning models when a suitable external teacher is unavailable. However, common approaches rely on privileged information to construct a stronger self-teacher, limiting their use when only unlabeled prompts are available. In this paper, we introduce Weakening-Induced On-Policy Self-Distillation (WISD), which instead constructs a weaker counterpart of the original model and uses the contrast between the two as a training signal without requiring privileged information. We explore two ways to construct the weaker model: low-bit quantization and magnitude pruning. We evaluate WISD across diverse architectures and scales on mathematical and coding reasoning tasks. Across these benchmarks, quantization and pruning improve average accuracy over the original models by 4.0 and 5.5 percentage points, respectively, achieving state-of-the-art performance even without privileged information. Notably, pruning raises Qwen3.5-4B's accuracy from 57.7% to 70.5% on HMMT 2025 and from 39.1% to 56.7% on LiveCodeBench v6. Quantization raises Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's accuracy from 68.5% to 74.6% and from 61.8% to 69.5% on the same benchmarks, respectively. These results establish WISD as a novel and effective self-distillation framework for large reasoning models.
  - *Impact*: Top community-ranked research paper (2 upvotes) discussing: Weaken to Strengthen: Weakening-Induced On-Policy Self-Distillation for Large Reasoning Models
- **🔥 [Unlocking Any-Order Generation in Pretrained Autoregressive Image Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39293)** (`2 upvotes`)
  - *Summary*: Autoregressive (AR) image generators are powerful in principle but inflexible in practice: training with a fixed raster-scan order hardcodes a single factorization of the image distribution into the model, leaving it unable to inpaint, outpaint, or edit at inference time. Existing solutions, including the modern discrete-diffusion family, train from scratch on a mixture of token orderings, paying roughly 3x the compute of standard raster-scan training; discrete diffusion further sacrifices compatibility with the AR inference ecosystem (KV caching, vLLM) by construction. We argue that this cost is unnecessary. A pretrained raster-scan AR model has already learned the joint distribution over image tokens; what it lacks is not knowledge but the ability to express that knowledge through non-sequential conditional pathways. We propose a recipe for adapting pretrained raster-scan models into any-order generators using two components: future-aware positional embeddings that resolve a target-position identifiability problem at negligible overhead, and an ordering curriculum that traces a stable optimization path from the pretrained solution to a good any-order basin. Applied to four base models across two architectures and two scales (LlamaGen-L/XL, RAR-L/XL), the recipe substantially outperforms MaskGIT on ImageNet inpainting (FID 6.21 vs. 10.44) and outpainting, is competitive with MAR-L, trades a 1.2-1.9 increase in class-conditional FID for this flexibility, and costs 13-33% additional compute beyond pretraining, while leaving the standard AR inference stack untouched. Inpainting, outpainting, and editing, our results suggest, need not be added to autoregressive image models. They can be unlocked from the ones that exist. Code is available at https://github.com/Ordered-AR/unlockedadaptation.
  - *Impact*: Top community-ranked research paper (2 upvotes) discussing: Unlocking Any-Order Generation in Pretrained Autoregressive Image Models
- **🔥 [Matched Capabilities, Different Behaviors: A Controlled Study of MCP and CLI Interfaces for Language Agents](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34298)** (`3 upvotes`)
  - *Summary*: Language agents increasingly act through external tools and interactive environments, making the interface between the model and its executable capabilities part of the decision process. Model Context Protocol (MCP) and command-line interfaces (CLI) can expose the same semantic operations through markedly different model-facing representations. We study whether these representational differences change agent behavior when executable capabilities are matched. We introduce Matched Interface Comparison and Analysis (MICA), which renders MCP and CLI from a shared semantic action contract and routes decoded actions through the same runtime. Across six models and three interactive environments, matched interfaces produce substantial differences in task performance and interaction cost. These effects extend beyond surface validity to semantic action selection, failure pathways, and recovery. Matched-state probes localize sensitivity to capability presentation, realization, and feedback. Paired natural trajectories reveal structured divergence in 94.3% of 2,004 episodes and show that early differences propagate through subsequent interaction. This analysis identifies interface realization as a direct intervention boundary. We therefore introduce Decoupled Action Realization and Targeted Repair (DART), which preserves validated semantic commitments while repairing interface-specific realization. DART improves benchmark score in all six evaluated settings by up to 9.0 points and reduces MCP token cost in all three environments by up to 27.1k tokens per task. Together, these results establish interface representation as part of the closed-loop decision environment shaping agent behavior.
